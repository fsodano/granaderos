# Operative portrait art direction and evidence

All thirteen portraits are original AI-generated paintings, made for the game's
1810–1820 setting. They are interpretive depictions, not historical photographs,
scans of museum objects, or authenticated reconstructions. No reference image
was supplied to the image generator; prompts used the specification, period
art conventions, and the contextual references below. Facial details without
specific documentary support are artistic choices. Clothes also need expert
historical review; visual consistency does not establish historical accuracy.

Research consulted 2026-09-05:

- [Museo Histórico Nacional: the face of Güemes](https://museohistoriconacional.cultura.gob.ar/noticia/17-de-junio-el-rostro-del-gral-martin-miguel-de-guemes/) explains that no portrait was made during his lifetime, and discusses later interpretations and written recollections. Our prompt uses dark curls, a curly beard and brown eyes from that discussion; it does not claim an exact likeness.
- [Argentina Cultura: San Martín portrait reconstruction](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/noticias/245-anos-del-nacimiento-de-jose-de-san-martin) discusses uncertainty and reconstruction across different ages. Our commander is interpreted at approximately 39, with familiar dark hair and sideburns, not the white-haired elderly image from 1848.
- [Museo Histórico Nacional: women in the revolution](https://museohistoriconacional.cultura.gob.ar/noticia/mujeres-recorrido-autonomo-tiempo-de-revolucion/) supplies context for Azurduy and Macacha, and presents museum portrait material. Their game portraits are younger wartime interpretations.
- [Cultura: the ideal portrait of Güemes](https://www.cultura.gob.ar/el-retrato-ideal-de-guemes-10657/) notes that the museum's Macacha miniature depicts her in old age. The game image does not pretend to be that miniature.
- [Museo Histórico Nacional: Time of the Provinces](https://museohistoriconacional.cultura.gob.ar/noticia/time-of-the-provinces/) catalogs portrait objects for Dorrego and Quiroga. Their later public images provide historical context, not a literal 1810s face reference.
- [Argentine Navy: Guillermo Brown](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/armada/historia-naval/heroes-navales/almirante-guillermo-brown) and [Hipólito Bouchard](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/235052) provide naval context. Our depictions avoid modern uniforms and fictional pirate motifs.
- [Patrimonio Argentino: José María Paz print](https://patrimonioargentino.cultura.gob.ar/index.php/Detail/objects/75365) identifies a later 1843 portrait; the game uses a younger interpretation, not the appearance of his later years.

Cabral, Beltrán, Paroissien and Barcala are explicitly conceptual individual
likenesses pending stronger portrait research. The game should not display any
claim that these images show the exact historical appearance of those people.

The shared art direction is warm traditional oil brushwork, a dark olive brown
background, close head-and-shoulders framing, period collars, no text, and clear
eyes and facial silhouettes that remain readable in small roster panels. Ages
are approximate wartime art choices. The source PNGs and exact individual
prompts are retained; browser copies are 384×384 WebP.
