## Before copying to other units

1. Obtain visual approval of each action at 1× in the fixed isometric view used by the game. Inspect other angles internally only where needed to diagnose grip or clipping. Do not equate passing tests with approval.
2. Check shoulder, elbow and wrist arcs throughout each clip, not only at impact. Check fingers and weapon grip at close range.
3. Armed movement and stepping attacks exist. Add and review starts/stops, reloads, crouching and reactions when required by the receiving unit.
4. Transfer motion intent, phase timings and body-relative targets. Recompute targets from the receiving anatomy; do not copy this adult rig's absolute metre coordinates blindly.
5. Refit grip/support sockets for each weapon. Preserve event semantics; presentation must not calculate gameplay damage or spend ammunition.
6. Re-export and repeat checks for each body/weapon combination. Keep this candidate out of the production shared animation bank until approved.

## Visual acceptance criteria

The user supplied a secondary AI description of animation principles. Treat it as a review checklist, not historical or biomechanical evidence:

- Support and weight transfer precede hip rotation; chest, shoulder, arm and weapon follow with overlap.
- Preparation is visible but compact. Knife preparation is smaller than sabre preparation.
- The blade accelerates through the cut; recovery takes longer than the fastest part of the strike.
- Track the wrist and blade-tip paths for continuous arcs, pauses and sudden direction changes.
- Hips, chest, free arm and weapon settle at different times; avoid a whole-body stop on one frame.
- Do not add large preparatory swings or secondary motion merely to make an action more visible.
