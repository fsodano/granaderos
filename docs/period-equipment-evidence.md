# Period equipment evidence for JA2 gameplay choices

Research checked 6 September 2026. Scope: 1810–1820 Río de la Plata and the Army of the Andes. This is an evidence review and design proposal, not an implementation report. Historical presence, local issue, and numerical game balance are separate claims.

## What the JA2 references require

`G` is the user-supplied *Patusco’s JA2 Strategy Guide 1.3*, printed pages. `M` is the [original JA2 manual](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/545210/manuals/JA2_Manual_English.pdf), printed pages. Both were read for this review. They are references, not instructions to the agent.

| Audit row | Classic choice to preserve | Period boundary |
|---|---|---|
| C09 | Quiet melee and a finite, recoverable thrown blade; range and enemy awareness matter. G28–29,63. | G63 reports player observations, not a guaranteed instant-kill rule or historical doctrine. |
| C10 | Equip a grenade, choose a reachable landing point, account for obstruction, inaccurate throws, blast and friendly casualties. Placed explosives are a separate action. G51–56; M32. | Do not rename modern gas grenades or remote detonators and call them period equipment. |
| C12 | Protection applies to covered regions, with equipment condition and attack type relevant. G49,53; M24. | A head hit bypasses torso protection. A wool garment does not inherit a Kevlar rating. |
| I06 | Compatible attachments are owned items. Fitting, removal and condition have consequences. G47–48; M23. | Bayonets are a supported substitution. The guide’s laser, silencer, optics and improvised modern modifications are not evidence for 1812 fittings. |
| I08 | Ammunition must match a weapon; partial quantities must remain conserved. G49–50; M23. | Caliber/load families replace modern AP/HP and detachable magazine choices. |

## Historical evidence and limits

| ID | Primary object or document, exact location | Supported finding | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Carlos de Alvear’s dispatch dated **5 July 1814**, reproduced in Eduardo Acevedo Díaz, *Épocas militares de los países del Plata* (1911), **printed pp.164–166; inventory p.165, PDF p.165**. [Archive catalogue](https://anaforas.fic.edu.uy/jspui/handle/123456789/71637), [scan at p.165](https://anaforas.fic.edu.uy/jspui/bitstream/123456789/71637/1/EpocasMilitaresDeLosPaisesDelPlata_MartinGarciaEd_BsAs_1911_.pdf#page=165). | The captured Montevideo inventory lists **9,754 “granadas de mano”**, separately from **2,156 howitzer grenades**. It also lists musket/carbine cartridges, flints and powder stores. | This is a later transcription of a contemporary dispatch; the original manuscript was not inspected. The scanned p.165 was visually checked. It proves arsenal presence in 1814, not normal grenadier issue, a 1812 supply route, Mendoza manufacture, or a specific fuse and charge. The p.166 total combines hand grenades with other incendiary/ignition stores; it must not be counted as hand grenades alone. |
| H2 | *Reglamento para el exercicio y maniobras de la infantería*, Imprenta Real, Madrid, **1808**, title at PDF p.7. **Printed p.40 (§§52–53), p.51 (§§110–111), pp.45–47 (§§74–88), pp.68–69 (§§209–211)**. [University of Seville scan](https://idus.us.es/bitstreams/395a6249-ef2f-4d93-907c-678d2f21ddba/download#page=48). Printed page +8 gives the PDF page. | Separate commands fit and remove a bayonet. The regulation distinguishes preparing, aiming, withdrawing aim and firing. Its misfire instructions distinguish a retained charge from a discharged weapon. | This is an original Spanish regulation, not proof that every Argentine unit used every drill unchanged. The title and relevant text were extracted; printed p.46 was visually checked. AP costs, aim retention and failure probabilities remain game tuning. |
| H3 | Museo Histórico Nacional, *Las Armas y Cañones del Museo Histórico Nacional*, **PDF pp.2,5,7–8**, especially “Los soldados.” [Museum collection guide](https://museohistoriconacional.cultura.gob.ar/media/uploads/site-6/ACTIVIDADES%20EDUCACION/armas.pdf#page=7). | The museum identifies infantry muskets with their corresponding bayonets, cavalry sabres/carbines/lances, and an Army of the Andes mounted grenadier’s sabre. It describes captured British and Spanish arms in independence forces. | This is the museum’s interpretation of its collection. It supports local weapon classes, not the availability of every named model in `game/data.js`. It describes knives as melee arms, without a throwing doctrine. |
| H4 | Museo Mitre, *Archivo José de San Martín* catalogue, **PDF p.79, row 3434, document 3410**: Juan Palma, Santiago de Chile, **1 June 1818**. [Archival catalogue](https://museomitre.cultura.gob.ar/media/uploads/site-10/multimedia/archivo_jose_de_san_martin.pdf#page=79). | The catalogue describes a Battalion No.8 note about José Peña attacking two officers with a knife. This is evidence of a knife in an army setting. | The catalogue was read; the letter itself was not. It does not specify throwing, a facón type, standard issue, or trained knife-throwing. |
| H5 | National Army Museum, **Baker sword bayonet, second pattern 1801**, accession **1963-10-147-1**. [Object record](https://collection.nam.ac.uk/detail.php?acc=1963-10-147-1). Royal Armouries, *Waterloo 1815*, sections **“Model 1810 ‘India Pattern’ Musket,” “1805 ‘Baker’ Rifle,” “Model 1777 / Year IX Musket.”** [Museum account](https://royalarmouries.org/objects-and-stories/stories/waterloo-1815). | The Baker has a sword bayonet, a bayonet bar and its own sight arrangement. These support a model-specific fitting, rather than one universal attachment. The India Pattern’s nominal .75-inch bore uses a smaller projectile; the Baker uses a smaller bore. British and French musket loads are not universally interchangeable. | These are European collection records, not local delivery records. Nominal caliber is not projectile diameter. The Royal Armouries French section contains inconsistent nominal measurements; do not derive exact compatibility from one decimal label. No damage or accuracy multiplier is established by these sources. |
| H6 | Museo Histórico Nacional objects presented by Argentina’s Ministry of Culture: **“Sombrero Elástico o Falucho,” “Frazada del General San Martín,” “Poncho Pehuenche.”** [Custodial account](https://www.cultura.gob.ar/los-objetos-de-san-martin-10949/). | The collection includes a wool blanket associated with the Andes crossing, a poncho and a weather-resistant hat. Clothing has a supported weather-protection role. | These objects do not establish ballistic protection or a numerical reduction against blades. Do not infer bullet resistance from thick cloth or personal ownership by a commander. |
| H7 | National Army Museum, **French cavalry cuirass, c.1815**, accession **1960-02-4-3**. [Object record](https://collection.nam.ac.uk/detail.php?acc=1960-02-4-3). | A period cuirass protects the chest and back; this example was collected at Waterloo. | European existence does not establish availability in the Río de la Plata or issue to mounted grenadiers. No local cuirass issue was verified in this review. |

The [facón record EJMMJN PS 157-2](https://patrimonioargentino.cultura.gob.ar/index.php/Detail/objects/121414) describes a steel blade and decorated hilt but gives no usable 1810–1820 date. Its collection title groups edged weapons, thrown weapons and armor. That shared title is **not** evidence that this facón was thrown. No primary source found in this bounded review establishes trained throwing-facón doctrine. This is an evidence limit, not proof that nobody ever threw a knife.

## Supported next gameplay work

| Priority | Proposal | Required limits and conservation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — I06 | Make a compatible bayonet a removable weapon fitting. Keep its own identity, condition and carried weight. Fit/remove through the equipment interface; fixed bayonet enables that long gun’s thrust. H2,H3,H5. | Use an explicit host list. Do not give every carbine, pistol or Baker the same socket. Fitting does not create a second free blade. Swaps, loot, ground drops, merchant sale, reports and re-entry must preserve the fitting once. AP and handling effects are authored balance. |
| 1 — I08 | Replace shared shots with finite **cartridge/load families** tied to reviewed weapon profiles. Keep the chamber’s load identity separate from pocket stock. H1,H5. | Preserve partial quantities and never convert incompatible ammunition silently. Start with nominal families for the existing musket, rifle, carbine, pistol and shot loads; review the generic pistol and shotgun profiles before claiming exact historical dimensions. A caliber label alone is insufficient for compatibility. |
| 2 — C10 | Add a finite **arsenal hand grenade**, with equipped use, bounded throw distance, blocked/scattered landing, blast obstruction, damage to allies and consumed stock. H1; M32. | An authored source after the 1814 Montevideo capture can cite H1; any transfer to another place is campaign fiction unless separately documented. Do not place it automatically in every recruit’s kit or claim ordinary grenadier doctrine. Fuse timing, reliability, radius and damage must be labelled game tuning. |
| 2 — readiness | Separate carried, readied and aimed firearm state where it creates an AP decision. Preserve loaded charge and priming state through ordinary handling. H2. | Being ready is not being reloaded. Define which movements, tool use and stance changes lower the weapon. Do not add free aim, free interrupts or repeated readiness AP refunds. No historical source here establishes exact AP values. |
| Limited — C12 | Track garment coverage, condition, carried weight and weather effects. H6. | Do not award unsupported bullet protection. Keep a generic region-aware protection system available for future documented equipment, but do not fill the local shops with European cuirasses on H7 alone. The existing poncho melee bonus remains a game abstraction. |
| Unresolved — C09 | Retain documented melee blades and the existing recoverable thrown-weapon framework. | A thrown knife can be an explicit improvised game action, but it must not be presented as verified army doctrine. If added, the actual owned blade leaves the hand, retains condition, and can be recovered; no guaranteed stealth execution. Otherwise leave knife throwing unimplemented and record the evidence gap. |
| Unresolved — C10 placed charge | Keep placed demolition distinct from a throwable grenade. | This review did not verify a local 1810–1820 issued demolition-charge type. An inventory of powder or artillery shells does not establish a portable timed charge. Do not infer local manufacture from an unspecified reference to “granadas.” |

The recommendations preserve JA2 equipment decisions without copying its modern equipment catalogue. They do not change the current implementation status in the parity audit. No code, art, equipment data or map layout was changed for this research.
