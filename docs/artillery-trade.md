# Stored artillery trading

The armory can sell an exact gun held in the local artillery depot, a friendly gun emplaced at the current workshop, or one undeployed cannon from available campaign/local depot stock. A deployed gun away from a workshop must first travel to one. All sold guns remain available for repurchase from that same workshop. Sale transfers the entire gun, including its identity, loaded charge, reserve rounds and unfinished reload. It does not create generic cannon or ammunition resources. Merchant-held guns no longer count toward the player's artillery.

The shop pays its local buying fraction (Retiro 40%, Caroya 30%, El Plumerillo 50% for artillery) and charges 80% for repurchase. See [workshop preferences](merchant-preferences.md). These are Granaderos tuning. Ammunition travels with the piece but receives no separate price. Merchant funds are finite. Purchases require actual player money. Trading requires a present squad, a supplied patriot workshop, no pending battle/encounter and no known active local hostile force. An emplaced gun requires its model’s full crew, alive and physically present, to deliver it. Each shop can retain 100 exact guns; each depot retains its existing 2,000-piece limit.

The shop retains sold guns across saves and stock refresh. A repeated sale or purchase fails without changing funds or custody. Save validation includes merchant guns in the same identity ledger as deployed, stored and transported pieces, and rejects invalid loads, cargo and duplicate ownership. Older saves need no invented merchant stock.

Validation includes payment/custody round trips, redeployment after repurchase, rejected orders, malformed saves and actual armory button dispatch. A separate local QA fixture passed live sale, reload/resume and repurchase: a bronze gun retained two rounds and 25% reload progress; treasury changed from 3,200 to 3,480 to 2,920 pesos, and merchant cash from 1,200 to 920 to 1,480.

Undeployed stock uses the same model allocation as tactical deployment: named heavy/swivel/bronze camp counts first, untyped camp guns as bronze, and only the current local depot’s generic bronze stock. A sale consumes one corresponding count and creates one distinct merchant gun with the existing deployment allowance of a loaded charge and six reserve rounds. A stock-count receipt rejects stale offers. An emplaced sale removes that exact piece from its tactical snapshot and strips only its old placement fields.

Five source tests and a render/input case cover all three purchased types, generic/local/remote depot accounting, retained field loading, crew/ownership rejection, stale offers, empty-stock bypass prevention and identity collisions. Before local buying preferences were introduced, an isolated live Córdoba fixture verified that an undeployed pedrero and an emplaced eight-pounder sold for 160 and 440 pesos. Both offers survived reload. Repurchasing the eight-pounder for 880 pesos retained its one reserve round and 60% partial reload. The fixture uses a scripted settlement to isolate trading, not to prove combat balance.

This does not close all trading requirements. [Workshop buying preferences](merchant-preferences.md) now affect the offer. [Equipment exchange](merchant-exchange.md) now combines artillery and handheld offers into one net payment. There is no remote sale or traveling battlefield merchant. The QA fixture establishes the interface and persistence flow, not a complete campaign or historical market-price accuracy.
