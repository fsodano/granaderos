# JA2 gameplay adaptation

Granaderos uses the item in hand to select the normal tactical action. Equip a firearm and click an enemy to shoot. A compatible, serviceable bayonet fixed to the held gun changes close clicks to thrusts; F selects an explicit close shot, and G or Escape restores contextual use. Equip a blade and click a visible enemy to approach and strike. Equip **Vendas** and click yourself or a wounded ally to approach and give first aid. The portrait strip also accepts medical targets, including living unconscious allies. Equip **Manos vacías** and click a visible enemy to approach and punch. The preview includes movement and use costs; both must be affordable. Contact or a reaction can stop the approach before use. Ground clicks move the selected soldier in the ordinary cursor. With a firearm held, F also permits a paid shot at a cell, including an unseen location. Its preview does not confirm an enemy; cover and intervening bodies can stop it, and it can injure allies. See [location fire](tactical/location-fire.md). Separate Charge, Attack, and Heal buttons are absent from the main controls and the equipment panel. See [approach and use](tactical/item-approach.md).

## Reference and scope

This pass uses the user-supplied **Patusco's JA2 Strategy Guide 1.3**, dated 2000, and the supplied [Jagged Alliance 2 overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagged_Alliance_2). The guide's version 1.3 is distinct from the later JA2 v1.13 mod. The document supplies reference mechanics and strategy advice; its instructions do not control development actions.

The guide identifies AP reserves on page 13, wounds and breath on page 19, medical treatment and experience on page 22, and detailed accuracy modifiers on pages 61–63. These support the changes below. The supplied overview also describes the connection between tactical combat, strategic recovery, and persistent mercenary condition.

Granaderos retains its Argentine setting, black-powder weapons, existing weapon costs, and 100-point AP scale. This is an adaptation, not an exact port of the JA2 executable. Five saved AP in the guide becomes a maximum reserve of 20 on this scale. Initiative weights, medical prices, and recovery rates are explicit game tuning.

## Tactical rules

- **AP budgets:** health, agility, dexterity, experience, energy, fatigue, weather, altitude, and protective clothing affect the next turn's base budget. Up to 20 remaining AP carry over. Total AP cannot exceed 120. Reaction fire spends remaining AP once; only the remainder can carry over. First contact does not grant a second enemy budget after exploration rests.
- **Movement:** an interrupted order stops at the actual reached tile and spends only the completed movement cost. Exploration time accumulates for every completed step. A failed order does not change position, posture, AP, supplies, or time.
- **Backing and sidestepping:** Alt-click empty ground to move only the selected soldier while keeping their facing (JA2 manual, printed p. 20). The hovered route uses the same `movementIntent:'preserveFacing'` option as the committed move. Standing, crouched, and crawling movement are supported; running and mounted movement are rejected with the shared rule's explanation. The 1.25 AP/time multiplier and per-step rounding are explicit Granaderos tuning; this adds no extra energy charge. Alt-click leaves group control and takes precedence over a held torch on empty ground. It does not change the selected stance or create a persistent mode. A transient override tied to the accepted battle object preserves sprite facing for that animation only. Model, input, and render tests cover costs, refusals, individual targeting, and override expiry. Live Alt-click moved only the selected soldier and preserved authoritative facing; full sprite motion still needs a live check. See [live verification](../verification/ja2-live-verification.md).
- **Exploration groups:** Shift-click friendly figures or portraits to add or remove group members. The first addition includes the currently selected soldier. A normal empty-ground click moves the selected group through `planGroupMove` and `executeGroupMove`, preserving each member's movement mode. The preview lists assigned cells, route lengths, and blocked members. Orders execute sequentially and report actual elapsed time; they do not simulate simultaneous marching. Contact clears the selection and restores individual control. Ordinary selection, number keys, Escape, and individual item orders also leave group control. An explicit group selection takes precedence over a held torch on empty ground; unit and visible door targeting remain individual. Model and render tests cover selection, shared plans, per-member results, and contact presentation. Live Shift-click and ordinary mouse movement reached the exact planned destinations. The preview now reserves its height so hovering a tile cannot move that tile before the click. Live first-contact stopping remains pending.
- **Posture:** standing, crouching, and prone are distinct. Each posture transition step costs 3 AP; standing directly from prone costs 6. A knocked-down soldier needs 12 AP to stand. Movement controls pay the same transition cost. Mounted soldiers must dismount before crouching or going prone.
- **Accuracy:** extra aim, shooter posture, distance-dependent target posture, weapon condition, wounds, bandages, energy, morale, shock, cover, smoke, light, and weapon range affect shots. Bandaged wounds retain half their wound penalty. Shock decreases at the soldier's next turn. A repeated shot at the same target gains a bonus; movement, looking elsewhere, and item changes clear it. Previews and execution use the same cost and accuracy functions.
- **Interrupts:** unused player AP can grant control during the enemy turn. New sight or sound, experience, agility, and shock decide eligibility. The eligible soldiers can use normal item, movement, and posture orders; they do not receive fresh AP. Continue resumes the saved enemy action queue from the reached tile without advancing the clock twice. Each soldier can interrupt once per round. Enemy reactions use the same remaining-AP actions, including movement and maintenance. One interruption can interrupt another; a saved reaction stack restores each suspended decision window and the original enemy queue. Opening a door can expose a threat. Anonymous hearing can grant control without revealing an enemy. Repeated unchanged sound reports do not grant another window. An enemy can choose to decline a reaction through its existing hold-fire policy.
- **Body locations:** firearm aim settings select torso, head, or legs without a separate attack mode. Head accuracy loses 3 points per tile and leg accuracy loses 1, before the weapon cap. Head injury uses a 1.5 multiplier; legs use 0.65 injury but more breath loss. A strong surviving leg hit can remove AP, knock down the target, and unhorse a rider. These damage/balance numbers are adaptation tuning, not a claim to reproduce the original executable.
- **Facing and hearing:** eight facing directions gate sight through a forward 180-degree arc; nearby awareness reaches two tiles. Looking costs 2 AP per 45-degree step, or 4 while prone. Movement and attacks face their direction as part of the action. Gunfire, movement, loading, doors, melee, and wire alarms make sounds. A listener receives a coarse area with uncertainty and no unseen unit identity. Walls muffle sound. Observation records each completed movement tile; enemies search the last observed or heard area rather than tracking hidden coordinates.
- **Stealth:** Z enables deliberate quiet movement independently of S/C/P posture controls. Stealth costs 25% more movement AP and time, while reducing noise according to skill. Mounted soldiers cannot enable it. L or a right click selects a paid look direction. A heard-noise marker shows the approximate area.
- **Contact:** either side can discover the other during exploration. Enemy-only discovery grants the enemy the first turn and stops a move at the discovered tile. When neither side has sight or contact from the last three turns, **Volver a explorar** resumes exploration with living enemies still present. Leaving combat preserves units, AP, equipment, and the clock; later real contact starts a new engagement.
- **Sector exits:** the existing **Retirada** / **Salir del sector** control opens a destination and participant panel. Soldiers must reach the exact authorized edge before crossing. The shared preview checks every selected soldier and rejects the batch if any member cannot leave; an explicit button selects only eligible members. Crossing spends the real outward-step AP, energy and time and can trigger enemy reactions. Partial departures leave the encounter active and keep receipts in the saved state, outside map figures, radar, roster, item targets and bandaging. The last capable player departure concludes a retreat. **M** suspends the view without withdrawing. The UI states that only one tactical encounter remains live. Model and server-render tests cover admission, costs, partial departure and visibility. Live checks verified boundary admission, one soldier crossing, reload with the same receipt, the remaining two crossing, and campaign arrival in Buenos Aires. Live enemy counterfire during crossing remains pending.
- **First aid:** a medical kit uses finite dressings and AP. It stops bleeding and records bandaged wounds. It does not restore HP. A conscious soldier with medical skill can treat themselves or an adjacent living ally, including an unconscious casualty. The kit must be in hand. Selecting it costs 4 AP, and returning to a weapon costs another 4 AP. These are separate actions: bleeding and interrupts can stop treatment after selection. Soldiers below 15 HP remain unconscious until campaign treatment raises their health. Dead soldiers do not revive.
- **Squad bandaging:** the medical area in equipment offers **Vendar escuadra** during safe exploration or after victory. A conscious medic uses their own kits, equips the medical item, and follows existing open paths to casualties. Bleeding medics treat themselves first. Each step uses the normal tactical action and clock; movement spends energy and untreated wounds continue bleeding. The operation stops on danger, exhaustion, insufficient supplies, or inaccessible patients and lists casualties still untreated. It never restores HP, rests, refills equipment, opens blocked routes, or resets AP. `tests/auto-bandage.test.mjs` checks ordinary-action replay, finite supplies, timing, critical casualties, blocked paths, medic collapse, and enemy discovery during travel. The equipment render test checks the optional control and untreated report; a live browser check remains pending.
- **Provisions:** eating restores energy and reduces fatigue. It does not stop bleeding or grant immediate AP. Untreated bleeding continues as tactical time passes, including during exploration rest.

Enemy decisions use current directional sight and short-lived observed or heard positions. Enemies can bandage wounds, reload, clear a failed ignition, aim, take cover, preserve firing AP, approach for melee, and search the last seen position. They do not follow the hidden target's current coordinates. Choices and tie breaks are deterministic.

Legacy `fire`, `melee`, `heal`, `charge`, `throwTorch`, `boleadoras`, and `ration` action objects remain supported by the simulation. Direct medical and supply orders require the same held item as `useItem`; they cannot bypass paid selection or restore a gun for free. Direct `heal` and `melee` remain local orders; ordinary player `useItem` can compose a paid approach with treatment or melee. The browser uses `{type:'useItem', unitId, targetId, aim, hitLocation}` for ordinary item targeting and `x,y` for a held torch. `{type:'weapon', slot:'primary'|'blade'|'medical'|'tool'|'supply'|'unarmed'}` changes the item in hand without losing the firearm's load. Tool and supply choices include their inventory key or supply key. The shared medical and supply previews also govern retained cursor modes and disabled HUD controls.

The AI uses observed opponents and recent sight or sound reports. Fire decisions compare head, torso and legs, affordable aim and damage after cover. They preserve AP when extra aim gives no benefit; see [AI shot selection](tactical/ai-shot-selection.md) for tuning and limits. It searches in short bounds of at most three steps and 24 AP per decision, while keeping AP for an attack. During an interrupt, a search allows at most one step and does not abandon cover for exposed ground. It stops to reassess when a path first gains sight around an obstruction. Unseen opposing positions do not change its planned route; actual collisions remain subject to the common movement rules. Blade troops use separate, paid movement and melee orders. These search limits are game tuning. Regression tests retain remaining AP across interrupted turns and replay the same legal orders after a save.

## Equipment and unarmed combat

The equipment panel provides item, quantity, and recipient controls. Adjacent soldiers pass items for 4 AP. Throws reach six tiles, cost 8 AP, and use the displayed catch chance; a successful catch costs the recipient 2 AP. An uncaught item remains at the recipient's feet. Dropping costs 4 AP and picking up costs 8. Reach, line of sight, remaining quantity, pocket space, and AP are checked before either inventory changes. The map picker supports individual checkboxes, quantities and Select All. One confirmed selection from one tile costs 8 AP; it fails without transfers or AP cost if the whole set does not fit.

Torches, boleadoras, and rations are held supplies. Select one in equipment or cycle with **W**; equipping costs 4 AP. The combat strip shows one supply slot. Click a map tile to throw the held torch (10 AP), a visible enemy to use boleadoras (12 AP), or the acting soldier to eat their ration (10 AP). Throwing reaches eight tiles and requires a clear path. A held torch takes precedence over movement and door interaction; select another item to walk. Rations only serve their holder, restore energy, and reduce fatigue. Empty stacks cannot be equipped. The equipment panel has no separate throw or eat action buttons. HUD and reducer share the supply-use preview; model and render tests cover costs, unavailable targets, interrupts, and item selection. Live browser verification of these controls remains pending.

Backpacks hold 12 capacity units. Equipped weapons use hands outside that budget. Supply stacks have explicit limits: 20 cartridges, 50 priming portions, 4 flints, 2 rations, 5 dressings, 2 boleadoras, or 2 torches per unit. Long arms use two units and compact weapons use one. This is an abstract adaptation of finite pockets; the original small/large slot geometry remains in the parity audit. Old over-capacity inventories retain their contents and can discard items, but cannot receive more until space is available.

Transfers, drops, pickup, and weapon swaps preserve loaded rounds, condition, a failed ignition, and an optional unique item identity. Swapping cannot overfill the backpack with the displaced weapon. Generic recovered objects remain valid items even when they cannot be equipped as weapons. Both backpack contents and loose sector equipment survive saves and re-entry.

Empty hands inflict limited health damage and greater breath loss. Skill, strength, experience, fatigue, and the defender's awareness affect a punch. A living opponent knocked unconscious can be searched for finite equipment. A held firearm uses its stock for an explicit close-combat order. Full bayonet thrusts and defensive bracing require a compatible, serviceable fitting on the held primary gun. A loose bayonet keeps its shorter improvised attack. B guards an installed bayonet; it does not install one. These values adapt JA2's unarmed decisions to the existing period combat rules.

## Doors, tools, traps, and containers

The inventory offers separate fit and removal orders for owned bayonets. The matched India socket variant fits only the Brown Bess. Generic legacy bayonets remain unidentified. Fitting costs 12 AP and consumes the selected loose source; removal costs 8 AP and requires either free backpack space or an empty secondary slot. The controls show the shared rejection reason, separate gun and fitting conditions, and fitting weight. A close contextual thrust uses the same resolver for AP, target text, and animation. A jammed or empty firearm can still thrust with a working fitting. Aim and shot-location modifiers apply to explicit shots. No fitting or combined attack button is added to the main combat bar. Model and rendered-control tests cover these paths. Live checks bought the matched item, fitted it, removed it to a pack, gave it to an ally, rejected the incompatible Baker host, returned and refitted it, then saved, reloaded and returned to campaign. Both an occupied-tile click and the corrected visible figure button produced the expected thrust. F plus the same figure button fired a deliberate close shot. The figure focus/click target now uses a fixed body ellipse instead of the clipped sprite atlas bounds.

The first matched item is `1811:india_socket`, sold from a separate finite stock of six per workshop for 50 pesos. Only firearm 1800 accepts it. Fitting moves its persistent identity onto the gun; removal preserves that identity and condition. A healthy fixed thrust uses 16 AP, 50 base damage and two-tile reach; each thrust wears the fitting by one point. Damage scales by `.5 + condition / 200`. A loose bayonet uses 16 AP, 24 damage and one-tile reach. These values are game tuning, not measurements from a drill manual. A broken fitting cannot thrust or guard. Firearm maintenance changes only the gun's condition.

The fitting weighs 0.5 kg whether loose or attached. Hands, packs and burden noise use catalog weapon weights, and packed loaded rounds retain their mass. A stowed fitted long gun takes two pack units; the attachment does not use another pack unit. The complete assembly survives giving, failed catches, ground pickup, corpse loot, rout, strategic swaps, sale, capture and departure. Save validation claims each currently owned identity once, while excluding historical deployment mirrors. Legacy unmarked bayonets do not acquire a pattern or an attachment.

Select a carried key, lock picks, crowbar, or pliers as the item in hand, then select a nearby door or chest. The contextual menu provides examination, opening/closing, the matching tool action, and bare force. Keys require the matching lock ID and remain reusable. Lock picks use mechanical skill, dexterity, experience, lock difficulty, and condition. Crowbars and bare force use strength and leave persistent lock damage. Opening, closing, examination, and key use cost 4 AP; lock picking costs 16, prying 20, forcing 24, and disarming 18. Lock picks and pliers lose two condition points per attempt; a crowbar loses three. Broken tools cannot work. Each tool occupies its own pocket unit; the larger crowbar occupies two. Transfer, drop, and campaign return preserve tool condition and the selected tool.

Examination can miss a trap. Its public preview does not reveal whether a hidden trap exists, its difficulty, or unopened contents. A discovered trap belongs to the observing side's knowledge. Equipped pliers and mechanical skill can disarm it; failure triggers it once. The authored Mendoza cache has a wire alarm with a 20-tile base hearing radius. The generic injury-trap prototype uses the shared wound and breath system, but no injury trap is authored on a map in this pass. Opening or manipulating a trapped closure spends the action and triggers the trap before opening. Once disarmed or triggered, it stays spent. These numerical rules are game tuning; the [classic manual, printed pages 25–27](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/steam/apps/545210/manuals/JA2_Manual_English.pdf) documents the underlying choices.

An unlocked chest at Yatasto holds one set of lock picks, one crowbar, one pair of pliers, and a key for the other entrance leaf. The cache can be reached through the open leaf without these tools. All four tools fit the five capacity units left by the normal supply load. Open a chest to view its finite contents, then choose an item and quantity. Picking up costs 8 AP and checks capacity before either side changes. The cache, depleted stacks, locks, and trap state persist through saves and sector reentry. Eighteen helper tests and ten actual-action tests cover these rules, including the legal Yatasto route and full campaign return. Contextual controls pass server-render checks; live UI verification remains pending.

## Campaign care

The campaign personnel panel provides **En servicio**, **Médico**, **Paciente**, **Descanso**, **Práctica individual**, **Instructor**, **Alumno**, and **Reparación** assignments. A doctor needs at least 20 medical skill, stable health, adequate energy, and a patient in the same safe sector. Each treatment hour consumes one of the doctor's kits. Bleeding is treated first; subsequent hours restore `2 + floor(medical / 20)` HP. One patient receives at most one doctor's treatment per hour. Doctors spend energy and gain fatigue while treating patients.

Rest restores energy and reduces fatigue. A stable soldier with at least 15 HP gains one HP per six hours of safe rest. Critical casualties need a doctor. Active duty and travel do not grant this recovery. Deployment, marching, and militia training prevent simultaneous treatment. Medical kits can be bought at a supplied workshop for 30 pesos each. These prices and rates are gameplay values.

Health, maximum health, bleeding, bandaged wounds, energy, medical supplies, and assignments persist in campaign saves. Deployment and return reports preserve medical consumption. Old saves gain the new fields without discarding their campaign or sector data. Invalid wounds, AP, postures, and remembered contacts are rejected.

Personal practice uses the existing `skillPractice` threshold of 40 points and `trainedStats` cap of 10 earned points per skill. It never edits the authored character profile. Zero-valued skills cannot be trained. Higher skills require more study time; wisdom improves the rate. An instructor must teach the same skill, exceed the student's current value, and remain available in the same safe sector. Each instructor has one student. Both spend energy and gain fatigue when instruction advances. Changing assignments, separation, exhaustion, or deployment pauses the work. Fractional progress persists in saves.

Repair assignments can service a living local companion's current carried equipment: secondary weapon first, then primary weapon and mounted bayonet, then pack weapons, fitted bayonets, lockpicks, crowbars and pliers. The personnel panel defaults new selections to this continuing queue and shows its next items. One shared hourly allowance of `1 + floor(mechanical / 15)` points covers the whole job. Each condition point costs one toolkit point, with fractional final damage rounded up; clearing a firearm jam costs one point and preserves ammunition. Old bulk stacks are split one unit at a time without changing quantity or pocket use. A 100-point toolkit costs 120 pesos at a supplied workshop. The queue follows what the selected person currently carries; equipment moved to the armory or another person is not repaired by that job. Movement, deployment, captivity, exhaustion, unsafe sectors or depleted tools pause work. Legacy gun-only jobs keep their original scope, and the panel still offers that explicit choice. Completion stops an explicit wait after the full working hour. The existing paid workshop service remains available. See [carried equipment repair](equipment/equipment-repair.md) for costs, save behavior and verification.

## Persistent morale and shared service

Personal morale is saved on a 0–100 scale. Initial morale uses existing character data: 80 normally, 90 for an optimist, 70 for a pessimist, plus 10 for `steadfast`, capped at 100. Tactical reports preserve the morale earned or lost during an engagement. A living deployed soldier receives +6 after victory, −5 after retreat, or −10 after defeat. Reports cannot be processed twice.

Losing a recruited squad companion costs 6 morale, plus `floor(sharedServiceHours / 40)` for that companion. The total companion-loss penalty is capped at 18 per report or strategic casualty event. This applies to battlefield casualties and deaths from untreated strategic bleeding. Dead soldiers do not gain morale. Historical profiles are unchanged.

Safe **Descanso** or **Paciente** assignments restore one morale every six eligible hours, only toward the character's initial morale. Eligibility requires at least 15 HP, no bleeding, and at least 60 energy. Active duty, marching, and deployment do not grant this recovery. A paid contract renewal or successful legacy monthly payroll grants +2 morale at most once per soldier per 24 hours; missed legacy payroll costs 10. Pay effects that occur during deployment remain after the tactical report returns.

Neutral cohesion records shared service between actual squad companions. Each hour together in a safe squad, outside travel and deployment, adds one shared-service hour. Completing a combat report whose tactical clock advanced adds 12 shared-service hours between its living recruited survivors. Each pair caps at 120 hours. The strongest bond with a living companion in the current squad gives `floor(hours / 24)` additional deployed morale, capped at 5. The deployment records personal morale and the actual bonus separately. Return reports remove that bonus, so repeated sector entry cannot turn it into permanent morale. The existing tactical accuracy rules use the resulting morale.

The guide's relationship discussion on pages 29–31 concerns authored likes and dislikes; its temperament discussion includes optimists and pessimists on page 33. Granaderos has no equivalent authored buddy or hostility records. Shared service is a neutral adaptation, not a claim about historical friendships. No recruitment refusal or invented negative relationship is added. Interpersonal-preference parity remains partial. All event values, recovery rates, service-hour thresholds, and cohesion bonuses above are explicit game tuning.

## Finite merchants and stored equipment

Retiro, Córdoba, and Mendoza have separate workshop stocks. Purchases and resale require a friendly, supplied workshop in the active squad's sector. Each local firearm model has at most three units, each blade model six, and each artillery model one. Ensenada has a separate three-unit order stock for each imported rifle. Each 24 hours of actual supply restores one unit per model up to its cap. Occupation, broken supply, or a battle at the merchant's sector pauses that clock. A blockade also pauses Ensenada's order-stock clock. Restoring supply does not grant missed stock immediately.

Workshops now hold at most 40 medical kits each. Buying kits spends treasury, credits the shop, checks pack capacity and decrements the real stock. Each supplied merchant day adds five kits up to the cap. Loss of control or supply pauses that clock. The purchase control can sell the remaining one to four kits as well as its normal five-kit batch. These stock and price values are authored campaign tuning. Ensenada is not a medical shop.

Imported weapons still require payment and 72–120 campaign hours for delivery. Blockade or loss of Ensenada delays delivery. Ordering consumes finite import stock immediately; pending orders do not put weapons into the armory. Each local workshop starts with 1,200 pesos for purchases from the player. Every eligible supply day restores up to 300 pesos toward that baseline; player purchases add their price to the merchant's cash. Stock and cash persist in saves.

Each stored handheld weapon has an individual ID, condition, and ignition-failure state. Swapping equipment preserves the outgoing weapon's condition and applies the selected incoming condition. An explicit stored ID allows replacement with another example of the same model. Stored guns are unloaded: campaign reports already reconcile cartridges separately. Selling consumes the exact stored ID and pays `floor(catalogPrice × 0.4 × condition / 100)` from the local merchant's cash. Weapons valued at zero have no buyer. Equipped weapons, aggregate model IDs, missing IDs, and replayed sales cannot be sold. Swapping or reporting a dropped weapon cannot create a replacement in storage. Legacy aggregate armory stock migrates to individual weapons at full condition, with strict count and ID checks.

Tactical weapon, blade, primary and blade condition, ignition failure, dropped state, selected hand, and optional item identities survive strategic return and later deployment. Every new battle or visit report, including mission visits, requires a complete tactical snapshot with the pending deployment, sector, and scene identity and a receipt for every deployed hired soldier, including casualties. Health, ammunition, and equipment come from that snapshot; caller receipt fields cannot replace them. Missing participants and repeated reports are rejected. A full tactical unit with no blade clears the old blade. Old-save migration can fill legacy fields after identity checks; it does not permit incomplete new reports or infer a physical departure. A gun retained only as a dropped model does not receive fresh campaign cartridges. Looted replacements remain equipped; the old gun enters strategic storage only through an actual armory swap, while backpack equipment remains in the backpack.

Medical purchases and provision refills use the same twelve-slot inventory accounting as tactical item transfers. Checks reserve the normal cartridge stack for the next deployment and reject the entire purchase before payment if the items will not fit. Automatic visit and attack allocations reduce spare cartridges until the pack fits, debit only the rounds actually issued, and can load the chamber separately. Existing overfull inventories remain loadable, but receive no new spare cartridges or further supplies. The personnel panel shows capacity and disables medical purchases that cannot fit. It also shows the finite workshop medicine stock and the next supplied-hour restock interval; the purchase button buys the remaining stock up to five kits, with price and pocket checks for that exact quantity. Empty stock disables the purchase.

Guide pages 69–70 discuss condition-sensitive trade and daily merchant cash; page 84 discusses waiting for a dealer's stock and money. Stock caps, refill rates, and peso values above are game tuning. Used-item buyback, dealer-specific preferences, and artillery resale remain partial: cannon ownership still uses the aggregate campaign resource system.

## Assignment time control

Explicit campaign waits stop when a current care, rest, repair, practice or militia assignment finishes or needs attention. An already blocked task is reported before time advances; a new hourly event stops only after every subsystem has processed that hour. The notice lists the affected assignments and actual versus requested hours. It survives reload. Pressing **Avanzar** again starts a new wait from the current hour without repeating an unchanged alert or automatically spending the earlier remainder. Rest includes its existing personal morale recovery. Assignments, supplies and progression remain governed by their ordinary rules. Travel and tactical synchronization keep their full durations. See [assignment time control](campaign/assignment-time-control.md) for evidence and remaining scope.

## Strategic enemy encounters

The former arithmetic raid result is replaced by persistent enemy groups. The existing 120-hour northern, 168-hour coastal, and 144-hour interior schedules now launch troops. The northern column follows Humahuaca, Jujuy, Salta, and Tucumán and stops at the first held frontier sector. Coastal targeting still responds to customs revenue; interior raids still target a vulnerable supply junction. Each group stores its command, route, progress, arrival time, seed, and individual soldiers. Route legs take 12 hours in the north, 8 on the coast, and 6 in the interior. These durations and the initial force of 3–30 soldiers are adaptation tuning.

Arrival at a defended sector stops strategic waiting and opens three choices: tactical defense, automatic resolution, or withdrawal to an available adjacent friendly sector. All hired soldiers present at that sector join its militia; the selected travel squad does not limit a defense to six people. A traveling squad finishes its current route leg before stopping. Arrivals during another tactical deployment wait until that deployment returns its report. A fort improves actual deployment cover and cannot defend an empty sector by itself.

Automatic resolution runs normal movement, attacks, maintenance, first aid, and interrupt decisions through the tactical engine. Fixed scouting waypoints search for hidden contacts without reading their positions. Ammunition, wounds, deaths, and equipment condition return from the actual battle. Resolution stops after at most 80 rounds or 600 control windows, then attempts withdrawal for at most eight additional normal rounds (also bounded to 128 control windows). Soldiers stand, free themselves, follow affordable path segments, and cross authorized edges through ordinary orders. Reactions, bleeding, AP and time remain active. If the encounter is still active, the result is unresolved (`outcome:null`), with its exact snapshot retained for manual control; it does not receive a synthetic retreat or victory. Automatic defense stores the synchronized tactical clock so re-entry does not age lights twice. It does not currently use explosives or offer the original per-portrait retreat controls.

Surviving enemy soldiers retain their health and equipment as an occupation force. A counterattack uses those soldiers instead of regenerating the sector's old enemies. Coastal defeat preserves civil ownership but creates an attackable occupation and blockade. Inland defeat changes control and can cut supply; partisan success also removes actual stores. Militia killed in combat are reported as casualties; surviving militia disperse on a lost defense. Tactical survivors change sectors only with a paid boundary-crossing record. Living personnel left on a lost field become prisoners; critical patients on a cleared friendly field remain resident. Captives retain their wounds, equipment and separate ammunition custody, and cannot work or be hired again while captive. Pre-combat strategic withdrawal remains a separate encounter response. Recapturing their held sector releases them and resumes the unexpired part of their contract. A contract that had already expired does not gain free service.

The guide supports consequential automatic combat and enemy pressure on valuable targets (pages 19 and 69–70); the manual's **Enemy Encounters** section describes tactical entry, auto-resolve, and retreat choices. This implementation adds real movement and encounter response. Independent scouting/intelligence uncertainty, interception between sectors, a full enemy reinforcement economy, and individual retreat orders during auto-resolve remain partial or absent. The five-phase production regression uses an existing-garrison fixture and scripted combat reports; it is not evidence of a complete manual campaign playthrough.

## Physical departure and persistent casualties

The existing retreat control opens a destination and soldier selection. Its preview names the blocked soldiers and uses the same checks as the exit order. A soldier must reach a passable tile on the designated boundary, be conscious and mobile, and have enough forces and combat AP for one outward step. A batch with an initially ineligible member changes nothing. During exploration, each crossing advances movement time and wounds; a collapse or renewed contact stops the attempt. In combat the crossing uses the existing six-second round clock and can provoke a normal movement reaction before departure. These exact-edge and cost rules are Granaderos tuning, informed by the original manual's tactical exit workflow.

All participants remain in the saved battle. A departure record identifies the actual exit, destination, boundary tile, time and mounted horse. Departed soldiers stop observing, blocking, acting and receiving item orders in the source sector. Their wounds continue to bleed while the rest of the encounter runs. The remaining group keeps tactical control; after its last capable soldier leaves, the result is retreat. A temporary breath knockout still counts as a recoverable person on the field. M preserves the current deployment. Following a departed group into a second live tactical sector remains unavailable in the current single-encounter architecture.

A morale break drops the held firearm once at the contact point. The soldier then uses ordinary movement, action points, forces and reactions on their turn. They stand before freeing entanglement if both conditions apply. A trapped soldier surrenders without an escape record. Fleeing people remain visible and vulnerable until they cross; surrender, departure, unconsciousness and death are separate states.

The complete campaign report partitions hired people into departed, resident, captured and dead. It splits original squads before assigning locations and preserves remote squads. Surplus destination groups become local reserves if all squad slots are full. Only an actually ridden horse follows a departure; other horses remain at their real location or in field/captive custody. Militia departures transfer their exact individual gear and rank count to the destination garrison. Only sixty militia deploy at once; stored reserves keep their finite equipment.

Living departed or friendly resident personnel return their actual ammunition once, under one shared issue-and-loot limit. That limit includes finite rounds recovered from older hired bodies, queued destination bodies, and living militia or mission allies; overlapping source records count once. Captive ammunition returns once upon release. Captive and departed equipment cannot also appear as source-sector loot. Corpses and abandoned equipment persist; a soldier who dies after crossing leaves their carried equipment at the destination, including militia and mission allies. A later arrival uses its authorized edge and faces inward. Only explicit residents reuse their prior location. An occupied or blocked arrival edge fails without placing the group inside a building.

Old saves gain authorized exit routes after their existing identity checks. A legacy fled flag does not create a departure. Snapshot validation checks route geometry, time, horse identity and reaction queues. Stored casualty records use a separate total-unit limit from the live combatant limit. An unresolved automatic battle passes its exact snapshot to manual control; the handoff cache is discarded on the first manual synchronization, including a same-clock action, so a later save retains the current battle. The tactical exit-order, route, world-entry, campaign-return, automatic-withdrawal and save tests cover these contracts. Live checks verified partial and complete peaceful exits, save/resume receipts, and the campaign destination. After equipped field aid, **Volver a la campaña** also recorded a cleared victory without withdrawing the resident squad. Enemy counterfire, routed exits and subgroup follow-through remain covered by model tests rather than live input.


### Cover and concealment

Firearm shots now check physical obstruction along their path. Furniture and window sills can protect legs while leaving a head exposed; shots through wood lose damage, and enough resistance stops them. The target preview warns when cover stops the selected shot or reduces its damage. A deliberate blocked shot still spends AP and its loaded round if ignition succeeds. Vegetation concealment affects sight and aim but does not absorb the bullet. See [projectile cover](tactical/projectile-cover.md) for current geometry, tuning and limits.

## Taking a held weapon

With empty hands, Ctrl-targeting or the pickup cursor can attempt a contested grab on an adjacent conscious enemy. It consumes all remaining AP, transfers one actual held weapon on success and preserves its condition, load, failure state and fittings. The displaced primary must fit in the pack. Ordinary targeting still punches. See [weapon stealing](tactical/weapon-stealing.md) for controls, explicit tuning and remaining AI limits.

## Militia combat experience

Militia now earn persistent credit for first wounds and kills against active opponents. Returned survivors can advance one rank while keeping their injuries and equipment. Veteran rank requires combat; new paid courses stop at the middle rank and preserve the actual trainees. The map shows rank counts and the campaign journal reports promotions. See [militia combat experience](characters/militia-combat-experience.md) for tuning, legacy-course behavior and verification.

## Enemy use of carried equipment

Enemies can put dressings in hand to treat nearby wounded allies, including critical patients. A short rescue approach uses ordinary movement and can be interrupted. They can also draw a prepared firearm from their own pack when the held weapon cannot deliver a useful shot. Swapping, fitting a bayonet, treating and firing all spend the same AP and finite equipment as player actions. Automatic defenders share the aid policy. See [AI field equipment](tactical/ai-field-equipment.md) for decision limits and saved-turn evidence.


## Contextual doors and containers

Select a visible door or chest to approach and use the current key or tool. The preview includes movement plus local action AP. Invalid tools and unaffordable totals reject before movement; contact, reactions and collapse stop before use. Unlocking, opening and disarming keep their separate physical effects. The nearby equipment panel remains available for explicit alternatives and finite contents. Keyboard map focus now shows the same preview as pointer targeting. See [environment approach](tactical/environment-approach.md) for rules, live key/disarm flows and remaining checks.


## Ground equipment and bodies

Selecting a visible ground marker or dead body approaches it, then opens an item-and-quantity picker. I uses the same search flow; Ctrl on a conscious opponent still attempts the existing contested grab. Body contents stay hidden until the soldier is within reach. Movement is paid even if the picker is canceled. Check individual rows or use Select All; each confirmed selection from the tile costs another 8 AP. A capacity or quantity failure transfers nothing. Recovered weapons keep loading, ignition failure, condition and fittings and enter the pack. See [loot approach](tactical/loot-approach.md) for control priorities, API behavior and verified persistence.
