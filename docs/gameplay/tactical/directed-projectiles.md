# Named-target shot paths

During tactical exploration and turn-based combat, a single-bullet firearm shot at an observed enemy follows a physical 3D trajectory on both a successful aiming roll and a miss, allowing intervening cover or bodies to intercept the ball and missed or penetrating shots to continue downrange. See [parity requirements](../../requirements/ja2-parity.md) row C04 (cover stops projectiles while concealment affects detection). The shot uses the same terrain and body traversal as location fire. Ordinary equipped-gun targeting is preserved; no new command or action button is added.

A successful aiming roll retains the selected target's head, torso or leg height. A failed roll offsets the destination cell using the existing seeded scatter rule and retains the original aiming height. The path can strike another living soldier, including an ally, surrendered soldier or unconscious casualty. A body can stop the ball or let it continue with less force. A failed accuracy roll excludes only the selected person's typed identity from incidental collision checks, preserving the existing miss result even if the coarse offset path crosses its cell. Other bodies and cover still apply. Dead and departed actors do not intercept it. Fallen and unconscious actors use prone height even when an older record still says standing.

Cover before the intercepted body can stop the shot or reduce injury. Cover after the body cannot protect it. Diagonal corner touches can strike cover but do not strike a body in the corner-adjacent cell. A descending close-range shot checks the whole crossed body interval, so it does not pass above a prone casualty merely because the midpoint of the last cell is still too high.

A single ball continues beyond the chosen or scattered cell in the same horizontal direction. Its absolute height keeps the original muzzle-to-aim slope through twice effective range, then drops on the shared deterministic quadratic; it does not turn toward a later person or adopt a later floor's height. Terrain, furniture, bodies and floor slabs are checked in intersection order, including after the aim point. Weak cover and successful body passage spend the same finite force budget; solid ground and floor slabs stop it. Each typed body can receive only one injury from that ball. The physical impact uses the actual intersection point rather than the centre of a person or obstacle.

Body passage is conditional. The default resistance is 15 force for a head, 30 for a torso and 23 for legs. Passage is impossible when that debit would exhaust the ball. Otherwise its seeded chance is `clamp(incomingForce - 20, 0, 95)%`; success spends the resistance and continues, failure stops at that body. A 52-force ball therefore has a 32% chance to pass a torso and arrives beyond it with 22 force. All injuries from one ball share the original damage variation draw. Cover damage tuning cannot restore force spent passing a body. Existing commander/bodyguard redirection remains, with actual damage recipients deduplicated so a guard cannot receive another injury from the same ball. These are editable Granaderos defaults, not historical lead-ball measurements.

The finite flight distance is the greater of the resolved aim distance and twice the weapon's effective range, clipped at the map boundary. This preserves existing legal shots beyond effective range. Effective range still affects the same accuracy calculation; it is not an admission limit. `firearmFlightRangeMultiplier: 2` is editable Granaderos tuning in [`game/combat-balance.js`](../../../packages/game/combat-balance.js), not a claimed JA2 or historical firearm constant.

## Knowledge, previews and AI

The preview includes friendly bodies and observed opponents only. Hidden bodies can intercept the actual shot but cannot change the forecast or expose their name through the journal. The forecast follows every possible body passage without drawing RNG. It multiplies the successive passage chances to report conditional reach of the selected typed target and its reduced incoming force. A warning still identifies known bystanders, including those beyond the selected target, and states that firing consumes the charge. The player can fire deliberately; the forecast is a possibility, not a promised downstream hit.

Enemy shot options use the actor's own sight for intervening opponents and retain friendly positions. AI rejects a potential ray that intersects a known friendly body before or beyond the selected target. A paired order checks both pistol rays. This does not guarantee that a random miss cannot injure an ally.

The bystander warning checks the same continued hit and scatter rays against known people. A person beyond the aim cell can therefore receive a warning. A failed roll excludes only the selected person's collection and identity: a civilian and soldier with the same ID remain distinct physical bodies. Hidden interceptions use an observed-body trace for the displayed fallback, so they do not expose the concealed body's position through the endpoint, camera or hit reaction.

If that fallback reaches a visible hard-cover stop, its existing material cue remains the same with or without an unknown person. If it instead reaches a known person, the fallback never invents that person's injury or hit reaction. Actual damage still uses the full physical trace.

AP, ignition failure, one finite loaded charge, firearm wear, smoke, noise, target memory, wounds, death, reactions and campaign time retain their common action pipeline. The journal reports a miss as failure to hit the chosen point, since another person may have been hit. Hidden injury and attacker names obey the journal observation rules.

## Source basis and limits

The released [classic JA2 source mirror, revision 876ccf5d](https://github.com/dariusk/ja2/blob/876ccf5dfdad7e6821c5b26d6d783132ea1ab7a2/ja2/Build/Tactical/LOS.c#L2106), and official [JA2-Stracciatella source, revision 04869c8e](https://github.com/ja2-stracciatella/ja2-stracciatella/blob/04869c8e339ead1de9fefb25b7ad206b9b786825/src/game/Tactical/LOS.cc#L2024), both implement conditional body passage with less remaining impact. This behavior predates v1.13. Their formula structure is behavior evidence; Granaderos uses its own force scale, weapon values, resistance defaults and bounded path geometry and continuous far-shot drop. It does not copy modern armour or special-ammunition rules. Classic and Stracciatella reduce the vertical increment beyond twice effective range in [actual flight](https://github.com/ja2-stracciatella/ja2-stracciatella/blob/04869c8e339ead1de9fefb25b7ad206b9b786825/src/game/Tactical/LOS.cc#L4217-L4223) and [forecasting](https://github.com/ja2-stracciatella/ja2-stracciatella/blob/04869c8e339ead1de9fefb25b7ad206b9b786825/src/game/Tactical/LOS.cc#L2839-L2845). Granaderos retains its finite flight distance and applies range-scaled drop on legal paths that extend beyond that onset. Its exact quadratic height clipping and arc-length material debit make this a gameplay rule; cannon and map graphics remain separately owned.

The scatter radius is `min(4, max(1, ceil(distance / 8)))`, with a nonzero cell offset. Cell-wide body silhouettes, abstract heights, fixed body-region thresholds, passage probabilities/resistance and the finite flight multiplier are explicit Granaderos tuning. Ricochet, vertical miss scatter and a supported mass/velocity model remain open ballistic work. Physical pellets share the exact two-range drop, with their separately documented finite three-range limit. The classic source review established no reflected flight; ricochet sound assets alone do not establish a physical bounce. [Physical shot loads](physical-shot-loads.md) resolve nine finite weighted pellet rays through the same ordered cover/body/ground traversal, not a per-person cone lottery. Their load-specific finite range and shared center/damage draws are separately documented; artillery retains its existing model. Row C04 remains partly open.

Presentation shows the ball's travel before the real downstream injury and holds input until one final commit. The downstream finite-force rules are described in [single-ball body penetration](body-penetration.md).
